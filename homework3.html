Reflection (about 250 words)

I tried to photograph the contents of a college bag, specifically what you might find in a girl’s bag. I want to showcase the everyday essentials we tend to carry and highlight how these small objects represent student life. Through this assignment, I learned the difference between shallow and deep depth of field and how each one changes the overall look of an image. A shallow depth of field allows you to focus on a specific element while keeping the background blurred, which helps you focus on it. In contrast, a deep depth of field keeps both the foreground and background in focus, showing the entire scene clearly. Aperture and f-stop played a big role in my results. Since I used a Sony camera and the lowest aperture available was f/4.5, that was the shallowest depth of field I could achieve. I learned that lower f-stops create more background blur and focus on the main subject, while higher f-stop makes more of the background visible and in focus. Because the room had poor lighting, I had to adjust the exposure carefully to brighten the image without overexposing it. In Camera Raw, I mainly adjusted the exposure and did not make changes to the histogram or clipping warnings. In Photoshop, I used the Lasso tool to select my subject and applied Field Blur to create an artificial shallow depth of field effect.
