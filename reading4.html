This article, “Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It” by W.J. Hennigan made me think about how serious the threat of nuclear war still is today. Many people associate nuclear conflicts with the past, like the Cold War, and assume the danger disappeared decades ago, but this article shows that the risk has actually become more complicated and unpredictable in the era we are living in. The article explains how tensions involving North Korea, China, Russia, Ukraine, and other global powers have made the possibility of nuclear escalation more realistic than many people realize. Something that stood out to me in the article was the detailed explanation of what would happen if a single nuclear weapon were used. The descriptions of the radiation, firestorms, destruction, and long-term environmental damage made the consequences feel real. Instead of focusing only on the political concept of nuclear war, the article focuses more on the human cost and suffering that ordinary civilians would experience. I think it is important that many people, especially the younger generation, learn about and become aware of the reality nuclear weapons hold. Since we did not really grow up during the Cold War, nuclear war can sometimes feel unrealistic, even though it is still a possible threat today. Another thing the article focuses on is how governments are preparing for the possibility of nuclear conflict. W.J. Hennigan explains how the United States and Ukraine created emergency response plans, radiation monitoring systems, and medical preparation programs because officials believe there is a possibility that Russia might use a tactical nuclear weapon during the war in Ukraine. This saddened me because it shows that governments are seriously preparing for something that could affect millions of people. It also made me realize that the danger is not just theoretical, but something world leaders are actively considering. The article also touches upon how fragile international peace really is. Nuclear weapons were originally justified as deterrents that would prevent war through fear of mutual destruction. However, as political tensions rise, technology advances, and the modernization of nuclear arsenals across multiple countries increases, deterrence is becoming unstable. Miscommunication, human error, or impulsive political decisions could potentially lead to catastrophic consequences. The thought of this is frightening because even if leaders do not want a nuclear war, it could still happen because of escalations or miscalculations between countries. Overall, this reading left me feeling both anxious and reflective. It reminded me that ongoing global threats continue to shape international politics today. It also showed me that nuclear weapons are not just historical artifacts from the past, but serious weapons that still pose dangers today. The article argues that the best way to prevent nuclear war is through diplomacy, arms control agreements, communication between nations, and public awareness. I agree with this perspective because ignoring the issue only allows the dangers to grow. Even though the topic is scary to talk about, I think it is important for people to remain informed and aware of the risks so history does not repeat itself.
