Reading and Written Responses

Reading and Written Response #3 : Laurence Ivil, Alicia Prager and Saidu Bah

The “Graphic Novel: Motherhood in Crisis” was very interesting and informative. It had four interactive stories that were almost comic-like, describing each individual situation. This presents a powerful and deeply unsettling view on maternal health in Sierra Leone. It reveals the reality of maternal health and how life-threatening pregnancy can become without proper care. The narrative is divided into four distinct stories that illustrate the systemic failures, poverty, and cultural stigma that create a crisis for these women. One of the most striking aspects of the reading is the extreme risk associated with childbirth in Sierra Leone, where one in ten women die during pregnancy or childbirth. This statistic alone is shocking, and the graphic novel goes further into why this happens. Limited healthcare, long and dangerous journeys to clinics or hospitals, and the lingering effects of crises like Ebola and COVID-19 all contribute to these issues. This opens your eyes to how much we take for granted having access to basic healthcare, while in other parts of the world it can become a life-or-death situation. The four women highlighted all faced different crises. Memunatu experienced giving birth alone because the nearest hospital was miles away. Due to the distance, many women have to give birth at home, and prolonged labor can cause stillbirths and uterine tearing. Heela’s experience as a young girl whose future is disrupted by pregnancy caused by sexual assault reflects how little justice is given to these girls, while the perpetrators run free without consequences. Kadiatu’s story centers on grief and the loss of her children. Many people have little faith and trust in healthcare facilities because, during the Ebola crisis, medical facilities became linked to fear and death in the eyes of the population. Tetteh’s situation, being imprisoned while pregnant, shows how social and legal systems can further marginalize vulnerable women. She accidentally killed her husband due to his abusive nature, while also losing custody of her child while in prison and not hearing from him since he was taken away. These stories demonstrate that the “crisis” is not just medical, but also social, economic, and political. I found the graphic novel format very interesting and significant. The visuals make the story more accessible and emotionally impactful while showcasing it in a comic-like format. Seeing the visuals of the environment, facial expressions, and conditions these women endure creates a stronger connection than just reading text alone. I feel that by combining art with journalism, the authors make complex global health issues easier to grasp while still portraying their seriousness. The way the stories were showcased highlighted themes of resilience, as despite the challenges the women faced, they navigated their circumstances and exposed the systemic failures that make such resilience necessary. Overall, the graphic novel “Motherhood in Crisis” was very emotionally impactful and eye-opening. It challenges readers to confront global inequalities in healthcare and consider the broader systems that shape women’s lives.
